# 2026-27 out of sample, through November 15

This page reports two periods, the first of which, October 20 to November 4 (121 games), is a replay with the data-path bugs fixed. November 5 to 15 (76 games) is the first forward test: everything was frozen on or before November 5, and injury reports and player ratings were captured as they were published. Every coefficient was fit on earlier seasons, but some rules were fixed only after opening night, and the table below says which. The live paper views that began on November 16 have [their own section](#live-paper-views-from-november-16), updated through November 22. Most of the other numbers are replays, for reasons given further down.

The moneyline rows are a pre-season model scored on a new season. For totals, the model was built during the season from earlier seasons and then run once on this one. The day-before spread rule is different: it was designed after looking at these same games, so it is in-sample. Player ratings come from an outside provider (DARKO) that revises its past values; the values used here were fetched on 2026-11-05 and may carry some hindsight.

## Summary

The model shows no forecasting edge: it trails the moneyline it is anchored on at every time of day, by amounts too small to be significant at 121 games.

The selection strategy bets only the games where the model is 2 or more points from the line. At opening lines it went 25-12, made up of 5-0 against the spread and 20-12 on totals. Priced at the first total our own poller saw on game day, totals went 14-13. On the games it bets, the model's log loss is better than the market's in every frame, and on the games it passes it is worse, though none of these differences is significant.

Player points are not ready: on 333 real lines the engine scored worse than the line, because it is overconfident. Rookie data is now complete, and all 53 rookies who have played carry a rating, although in past seasons a rookie's two-week rating said little about where he finished.

The sample is small throughout: forty bets cannot separate skill from luck, and the opening prices were not captured live.

## Totals: line movement and stake size

The full list of strategies, with results by month and the explanation for each, is in [STRATEGIES.md](STRATEGIES.md). The numbers in this section come from the 2012-26 walk-forward backtest of the totals rule, which bets over or under when the model's gap from the opening total is 2 or more points. They are from earlier seasons, and they assume the opening number can be bet at -110.

### The edge dies when the line moves

The table splits the model's bets at a 2+ point gap, priced at the closing line, by what the line had done since it opened.

| Where the line was | Cover rate | Bets | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Within 1 point of the opener | 54.9% | 344 | +4.9% |
| Moved 1+ points with the model | 52.0% | 1,162 | -0.8% |
| Moved 1+ points against the model | 48.1% | 549 | -8.2% |

This season agrees, on a small sample: bets where our first look was still the opener have gone 9-6, and bets where the line had already moved 12-14.

The live rule compares the model with the line on screen. It therefore bets bigger when the line has moved against it, which is the losing case. A second arm that bets only within 1 point of the opener was registered on 2026-11-16, before any live bet existed, and it will be compared with the current rule at 60 bets. The split above was found after the model's gate, so for now it is a hypothesis.

Two related ideas were checked and rejected: betting against large line moves at the close covers 48 to 49% and betting with them covers 51 to 52%, so line moves do not overshoot.

### Returns by stake size

The backtest holds 2,057 bets over 14 seasons at a 54.5% cover rate. Stakes are a fixed share of the current bankroll, capped at 25% of bankroll at risk on one night. Kelly is the stake that maximises long-run growth for a known edge, which is 4.5% of bankroll here, and the stake for each season is set from the cover rate of earlier seasons only.

| Stake | Mean season return | Median | Volatility (sd) | Worst season | Losing seasons | Largest drawdown |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Flat 1% of starting bankroll | +6.1% | not computed | 7.5% | -6.2% | 3 of 14 | not computed |
| Quarter Kelly | +6.2% | +6.7% | 10.4% | -11.2% | 3 of 14 | 26% |
| Half Kelly | +11.2% | +12.6% | 22.1% | -26.6% | 3 of 14 | 48% |
| Full Kelly | +17.3% | +22.4% | 47.1% | -53.4% | 3 of 14 | 71% |

One season stands out: 2021-22 returned +29% at quarter Kelly, and no other season reached +17%.

Full Kelly is not usable, because it assumes the edge is known exactly, whereas here it is an estimate from a backtest, and at its worst point it loses about 70% of the bankroll.

Per bet, the return has a standard deviation of 0.95 units against an average of +0.041, which is why a season of 147 bets is this noisy. Per-season results for every stake size are in `data/season_2026_27/totals_bankroll_backtest.json`.

## The live book was broken

The live paper book ran from opening night, but an audit on 2026-11-05 found that it was not running the system the backtests describe.

There were five faults, the first being that the injury-report poller looked for a file name the league stopped using in January 2026, and it captured nothing all season. Player ratings were frozen at their July values, with every rookie priced as a league-average player. Players who had left a team years ago were counted as absent from it, which made one betting rule fire on absences that were not real. Jobs were scheduled in the wrong time zone, so two of the three paper views priced games after they had tipped. The closing price used for line-value scoring was the entry price itself.

All five are fixed, and the numbers below re-price each game with the corrected code, using what was published before the price it is compared with: tables as of that morning, and injury reports stamped before the quote. No coefficient was refit, and the live path as it actually ran is shown once, under the first table, which puts the cost of the bugs on the record.

### Totals, 121 games

A totals model was added this season to predict how far the final total will land from the opening number. Its nine inputs are all known when the total opens, and most of them describe how the market has recently priced these two teams and the league. It was fit on 2007-08 to 2025-26 and frozen, and in the pre-season walk-forward (2012-26) its over/under picks at a 2+ point gap covered 55.3% on 2,380 bets. That walk-forward let the model see the previous night's results, which a bet at the opener cannot; without them the figure is 54.5% on 2,057 bets. That figure is from a rerun after the 2024-25 opening lines were repaired (see the [engine page](ENGINE.md#a-repair-to-the-2024-25-opening-lines)); the frozen coefficients were fitted before the repair and differ slightly from a refit, moving this season's predictions by 0.08 points on average.

## Selection strategy

The strategy bets only when the model's predicted gap from the line is 2 points or more. A flat stake is 1 unit, and a sized stake is the gap divided by 2, capped at 2.5 units, on the same bets. Totals are priced at -110, and spreads use the best number among the books quoting.

| Rule and price | Record | Units, flat | ROI, flat (95% CI) | Units, sized | Line moved our way |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spread, gap 2+, first line the day before | 5-0 | +4.5 | too few bets | +6.2 | +2.9 points |
| Totals, gap 2+, DraftKings' opening total | 20-12 | +6.2 | +19% (-16%, +49%) | +11.5 | +1.5 points |
| Totals, gap 2+, first total seen on game day | 14-13 | -0.3 | -1% (-36%, +34%) | +1.3 | 0.0 points |

Other cuts of the same games agree: totals at a 3+ point gap against the opener went 6-1. Spreads at a 1.5+ point gap went 10-6, for +3.1 units, while spread games with a gap under 1.5 points, which are not bets, went 48-47. By week, totals against the opener went 9-7 through October 27 and 11-5 after.

### Player points

This was the first test of the player engine against real lines: 333 graded player-points lines from 28 games, taken about 4 hours before tip with 3.8 books per line. We sample only about two games a night, and the historical lines needed for a backtest are not available free.

The engine scored a log loss of 0.7147, and with the line at 0.6904 and a coin flip at 0.6931, the engine trails the line by +0.0243 (95% CI -0.0081, +0.0572) and is worse than a coin flip, because it is overconfident. Its average miss was 5.20 points against 5.02 for the line. The hold is still 4.33% after taking the best over and the best under.

| Engine vs line, in probability | Bets | Hit rate | ROI at the best price (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5+ points | 238 | 53.4% | +2.3% (-9.7%, +14.9%) |
| 10+ points | 151 | 54.3% | +4.2% (-11.2%, +19.4%) |
| 20+ points | 49 | 65.3% | +26.6% (-1.0%, +53.4%) |

Results improve as the disagreement grows, but every interval includes zero. The thresholds were chosen after the engine had already run, and the best price is the best among the books quoted at that moment, so the engine is not bettable as it stands.

## Rookies

Before the 2026-11-05 fix, every 2026-27 rookie was priced as a league-average player. All 53 rookies who have played now have a rating, which covers 100% of rookie minutes, and rookies play 6.9% of all minutes.

The 23 in a rotation (3 or more games of 12+ minutes) have a median of 8 games and 8 rating updates; their ratings average -1.61 and have moved 0.80 points on average since their first one. A rookie with no rating yet receives a default by draft slot, which runs from -1.58 for top-5 picks to -2.43 for second-round picks.

The table shows how far a rating taken two weeks into a season can be trusted, using 2022-23 to 2025-26.

| | Players | Correlation with 2 months later | Correlation with season end | Average change by season end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rookies | 225 | 0.51 | 0.32 | +0.56 |
| Veterans | 1,749 | 0.94 | 0.87 | +0.04 |

Rookie ratings stay rough for a while, and they tend to start too low. That matters most for the few teams that lean heavily on rookies. The rating provider revises its past values, so these correlations may look somewhat better than what was knowable at the time.

## Caveats

The totals result is a clean out-of-sample test of a frozen model. Even so, 32 bets cannot tell 63% from the 55% the backtest suggests, and the ROI interval includes zero. The spread result is five bets, on dates that were looked at while the day-before rule was being designed, so it carries little weight.

The edge is at the opening price, and it does not last. On 2026-11-05, two of seven next-day totals had moved 3 to 5 points toward the model before a 30-minute poll first saw them. No opening total was captured live before that date, so the opening-price rows assume a fill that has not been demonstrated; the game-day row is what the system could have bet, and it is break-even.

Log loss and the strategy measure different things: the model does not forecast better than the market on average. The strategy bets the few games where the two disagree most, at the first price.

Both rules now run as pre-registered paper views that record the price on screen, the opening price and the close, which will answer the capture question directly as the season goes on. No capital has been deployed on either.
